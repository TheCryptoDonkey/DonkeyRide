How a job runs

Three steps, and no account at any of them.

STEP 01

Ask

Open the app and set where you're going. You see the price before you request it, broken down to the penny. Your phone makes its own key — there is no sign-up, no phone number and no card on file.

STEP 02

Match

Nearby drivers see the job and choose it — nothing is assigned to anyone. You each see who the other is, and the rating they've earned, signed by the people who actually rode with them.

STEP 03

Pay the driver

At the end you pay the driver, not a platform. Cash in hand, M-Pesa, or Lightning straight to their wallet. The price is the one you were quoted at the start.

Straight answers

What this is, and what it isn't yet.

Drivers have been promised a lot by companies that meant none of it. So here is the boundary, in the same place as the pitch.

What works today

  • A live demo you can use now — request a ride, drive one, get paid, rate each other.
  • A signed Android driver app, downloaded straight from the operator, with background location so jobs arrive with the screen off.
  • The rider app in any browser. Add it to your home screen and it behaves like an app.
  • The price you're quoted is the price recorded. One routing and pricing path serves both.
  • Ratings you carry with you. They're signed by the rider on a public network, so they're yours — not an operator's to hold hostage.

What it isn't

  • Not a company. There's no head office and no support desk. Somebody runs an operator; the code is the product.
  • No iPhone driver app yet. It builds, but it hasn't been proven on real hardware and can't alert you when you're off shift.
  • The operator forgets you. Exact addresses live in memory and are gone on restart — deliberate, but it means no operator can produce your history later.
  • It holds no money, so it can't refund you either. A fare is settled between two people who met.
  • Licensing is yours. Private hire rules don't go away because the software is open — an operator has to meet them.
